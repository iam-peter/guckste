Guckste

Lossless stills

Screenshots taken while you record: the exact pixels of a moment, kept when you ask, around every click or whenever the screen settles on something new, and found again on the editor’s timeline.

Why not just take a frame of the video?

A video is compressed while it is recorded. Each frame loses most of its color detail and gets the encoder’s traces around sharp edges, so a frame taken out of the video afterwards is soft, however high the quality. Text and thin lines suffer most.

Stills are kept before that happens: the frame exactly as it was on screen, written as a PNG next to the recording. The video stays as it is.

Four ways to keep stills

All of them work while a recording is running and keep the moment of the region being recorded.

The camera button

The small panel beside the recorded region has a camera button. Each click keeps the frame shown now, and the panel counts them.

A key of your own

Bind guckste still to a key in your desktop’s keyboard settings, as you would guckste record. Recording a whole screen has no panel; there the tray menu has Keep a Still as well.

Every click

Turn on Keep lossless stills before and after each click while recording in the settings, and go through the flow as usual. Two stills come with each click.

Every change

Turn on Keep lossless stills of what the screen settles on after it changes. No click needed: a tooltip, a hover highlight or a new page each give a still once they have settled.

Stills on every click

Clicking through a flow already marks its steps: each click leads from one state of the screen to the next. So for each click inside the recorded region, Guckste keeps

  • the still before it, the last frame before the button went down. The pointer is already over what it clicks, so the button’s hover state, a highlighted row or an open menu are in it.
  • the still after it, once the screen has stopped changing: what the click led to, after any animation has played.

The listening is the same as for showing keys: XInput2 on X11, a mouse hook on Windows, an event tap on macOS, which asks for the Accessibility permission. Wayland has no way to listen, so the setting isn’t there; the camera button and guckste still work everywhere.

When is the screen “settled”?

  • A click that changes nothing within 2 seconds keeps only the still before it.
  • When the screen never settles, as while a video plays, the still after is taken 4 seconds after the click.
  • Clicking again before the screen has settled ends the first click there: what it led to so far is its still after.
  • A still showing the same as the one kept before it is left out, so a double click or a row of clicks on nothing doesn’t pile up copies. The camera button and guckste still always keep one.
  • Clicks outside the region, like on the recording panel, don’t count.

Stills on every change

Some states come without a click: a tooltip after resting the pointer, a row lighting up under it, a page loading on its own. With this setting on, Guckste compares each frame with what the screen last settled on. When as many pixels as a tooltip covers have changed, it waits until the screen stays the same for 0.3 seconds and keeps that frame, the same way as the still after a click.

  • Small changes don’t count: a blinking caret or a ticking clock are too few pixels to keep a still, even both at once at opposite corners. Changes add up, though: typing a line gives a still once you pause.
  • An animation is kept once, when it ends. Something that never stops moving, like a playing video, gives a still every 4 seconds.
  • While a click waits for its still after, the click’s stills stand for the change it makes; there is no extra one.
  • The pointer is not part of the picture, so moving it around keeps nothing by itself; only what the screen does in response.
  • No listening is needed, so it works on Wayland as well. Both settings can be on together.

Where they go

Every recording gets a folder of its own, so a flow stays together: the video, and its stills beside it, numbered in the order they were kept.

When a recording opens in the editor first (the default), it waits in a temporary folder while the editor is open. Saving the video puts it into the recording’s folder and copies the stills of the part kept beside it; the image button on the toolbar saves the stills alone. Closing the editor without saving deletes the recording and its stills, after asking.

Only after the stills? Turn on Keep only the stills of a recording, without the video. Nothing is encoded then, the folder holds just the stills, and stopping opens them in the editor as one picture.

The stills are without the pointer. Where it was is kept with them, so the editor can put it back as a layer of its own.

Finding them in the editor

Each still is a small diamond on the top edge of the timeline:

before a click after a click after the screen changed kept with the camera button or a key
ToDo
Go to a stillClick the timeline near its diamond: within a few pixels it snaps to it. Hovering shows its time and kind.
Step through themCtrl+. goes to the next, Ctrl+, to the one before.
See itWith the playhead on a still the canvas shows the still itself, with the pointer painted in, instead of the video’s frame.
Open it as an imageThe camera button on the toolbar, or Ctrl+Shift+F. Its tooltip says “Open this lossless still” when the playhead is on one.
Copy itCtrl+C copies it, pointer and all.

The diamonds belong to their clip: trim its start or move it in time and they go along; trim a still away and its diamond goes too.

Opening one

The still opens in a new editor at its full quality, like any screenshot: draw on it, crop it, combine it with others, copy or save it. The pointer is a separate layer named Pointer above it. Keep it to show where the click went, drag it somewhere else, or delete it for a clean picture. Marks drawn on the video at that moment come along, still editable.

The whole flow as one picture

The grid button on the toolbar opens the stills between the timeline’s handles in a new editor, in rows of three in the order they were kept, each with its pointer as a layer of its own. Number the steps, draw arrows between them, delete the stills you don’t need, and save the flow as one image. Trim the timeline first to pick a part: up to 24 stills go into one picture, fewer of a 4K screen.

Good to know

  • For a state that only shows while hovering, a tooltip for example, turn on stills on every change, or rest the pointer there and press your guckste still key.
  • Stills show what was on screen. The keys shown along the bottom of a recording are left out of them, and so are the red frame and the panel, which are outside the region.
  • A pause stops the stills too; their times are the video’s, without the pause.
